The Argument

“Miami’s early growth was not simply a story of boosterism and real estate fantasy. It was built through immigrant labor, racialized urban planning, and unequal access to land, housing, and mobility.”

Black Bahamian and African American communities were foundational to Miami’s development long before the more commonly referenced post-1959 immigrant era. Census data from 1910 and 1920 helps quantify these changes and reveals a city whose ‘Magic City’ mythology obscured the labor and segregation that made it possible.
