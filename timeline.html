Chronology

A Timeline of Growth, Labor, and Division

1836

Dade County Incorporated

Named after Brevet Major Francis Langhorne Dade, killed in a Seminole ambush. The county would encompass the vast, sparsely populated southern tip of Florida for decades.

founding

1880s

Flagler's Railroad Begins

Henry Flagler, co-founder of Standard Oil, begins building his Florida East Coast Railway and hotel empire, reshaping entire towns along the coast. Black workers are pushed into segregated settlements.

development

1896

City of Miami Incorporated

Julia Tuttle convinced Flagler to extend his railroad to Miami by sending him blooming orange branches after the Great Freeze. Black Bahamian voters helped meet the incorporation threshold. The Royal Palm Hotel opens, but Colored Town receives no city services.

founding

1900

Census: Population 3,888

Dade County records its first substantial census count. The population is small but the foundations of a booming frontier economy are being laid through railroad expansion and land speculation.

development

1910

Census: Population 11,933

A 207% increase in one decade. Miami is dubbed 'The Magic City.' Over 35% of the population is Black, largely Bahamian immigrants and African Americans who built the city's infrastructure. Foreign-born whites make up 9.4% of the white population.

development

1910s

Bahamian Labor Migration Peaks

Black Bahamians arrive seeking economic opportunity but encounter American segregation for the first time. They find lodging in Colored Town's 'unpainted, poorly ventilated rooming houses' — 'a filthy backyard to the Magic City.'

labor

1916

Dana Dorsey Founds Negro Savings Bank

Miami's first Black millionaire, Dana A. Dorsey, establishes the Negro Savings Bank so Black residents would have 'a place of their own to bank.' He also builds the first Black-owned hotel in Miami.

labor

1918

Dorsey Purchases Fisher Island

Dorsey buys 21 acres near Miami Beach to create oceanfront access for Black residents, who were forbidden from public beaches. Rising land values during the 1925 boom force him to sell to Carl Fisher's company.

segregation

1920

Census: Population 42,753

A staggering 258% growth in a single decade. Dade County's share of Florida's population nearly triples from 1.6% to 4.4%. Over 55% of homes are rented — a signature of transient labor and speculative growth.

development

1920

KKK Bombs Colored Town

Two dynamite bombs explode in Colored Town. The following year, the Klan parades publicly through Miami and kidnaps Reverend H.H. Higgs, a Bahamian minister who preached racial equality. No perpetrators are punished.

segregation

1920s

The Land Boom

Real estate speculation reaches a fever pitch. Property values skyrocket. The color line hardens through KKK activity, union exclusion, nativist immigration politics, and restrictive covenants. Miami Beach is staged as a resort built on commuter Black labor.

development

1926

The Great Miami Hurricane

A devastating hurricane destroys much of the city. It exposes who holds power: Black residents are conscripted into forced cleanup labor. The city pivots to Prohibition routes, aviation (Pan Am), and light industry.

crisis

1930s

Depression and Reinvention

Despite the Great Depression, Miami experiences unusual construction growth. Hundreds of Art Deco hotels rise on South Beach. Jewish and Latin American newcomers reshape ownership and commerce, but the color line holds firm.
